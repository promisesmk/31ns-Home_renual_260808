PCB 3D Builder
A new dimension for your PCB designs.

A Windows program that builds a 3D STEP model of a populated PCB from a single PADS ASCII (.asc) file.
Without an ASC file, it can also work from DXF plus a placement file, or from board dimensions.

01 / Why it matters

More dimension.
Less manual work.

If you have PCB design data but no 3D model with the parts placed, working with mechanical design may mean ordering separate modeling or doing extra work yourself.

  • No 3D model yet

    Design data alone gives mechanical design no 3D model to work with, so separate modeling or extra work may be needed.

  • Finding and fitting every part

    Finding a 3D model for each part, then placing and orienting them one by one, takes time and effort.

  • From design data to STEP

    PCB 3D Builder uses your design data and parts library to help build the model, check and adjust it, and hand it over as STEP. It helps reduce the time and cost of separate modeling.

The model is reference material for design review; mechanical design and a final review are still needed.

03 / Features

Models filled in.
Less work by hand.

  • Model Matching

    Model matching · auto alignment

    Parts already registered in the library are reused; otherwise parts are filled with real KiCad models, then standard shapes, then simple pad-based models. STEP models are automatically turned to the orientation that best fits the pads, and with ASC input IC pin 1 and diode polarity are aligned too. Automatic results are a starting point for checking and adjustment.

  • From Datasheet

    Part 3D from a datasheet PDF

    Reads the dimension table in a package drawing PDF and builds the part as one of seven shape templates. Review and correct the dimensions it reads, check the preview, then register it. Without a PDF, dimensions can be estimated from the pads or entered manually.

  • Check & Adjust

    Part check & adjust

    Shows the selected part in top, fixed-angle 3D and front views against its pads, pin 1 pad and silkscreen, and lets you rotate it by 90° or 180°, enter coordinates, or drag it into place. You can also rerun auto alignment or replace it with a temporary box.

  • Board Preview

    Board preview

    For the whole board, switch between the top view and the bottom view (mirrored left to right), with front and side views alongside. Zoom into an area and return with a click or Esc.

  • STEP Options

    Board surface · color · labels

    With ASC input, the STEP can include silkscreen, copper and traces. Choose the PCB color and whether to engrave part names (none · selected · all).

  • Height & Report

    Height check · review report

    Enter the enclosure’s inner height to outline taller parts in red. The Excel report has separate sheets for the placement list, parts without 3D (including temporary boxes), height overruns and parts outside the board outline, gathering what to check after the model is built.

07 / FAQ

Questions before you start.
Answered one by one.

01Which design files do I need?＋

A single PADS ASCII (.asc) file is recommended. Without ASC, use DXF (top · bottom) and, if needed, a placement file (Excel · CSV). A BOM, part STEP · ZIP files and datasheet PDFs are optional extras that help.

02Can I use it with only a DXF?＋

Yes, if it is a PADS DXF that contains part information such as reference designators and footprints. Other DXF files need a placement file for the parts to be placed. With DXF, the STEP contains the bare board without silkscreen or copper.

03Is a BOM required?＋

No, it is optional. It improves finding part model candidates by part number.

04Are all parts built accurately and automatically?＋

No. 3D auto-fill uses real KiCad models, then standard shapes, then simple pad-based models and aligns them to the pads, but some parts may remain simple models or temporary boxes, or be marked as needing a check. Review them in the parts list’s 3D status and the check & adjust view, and register a STEP or adjust them yourself where needed.

05When can a datasheet PDF be used?＋

When the package drawing’s dimension table can be read as text and the part is one of the seven supported shapes (two-sided gull-wing, four-sided gull-wing, no-lead, chip, diode, cylindrical, box). Review and correct the dimensions it reads, check the preview, then register. Register other parts with STEP · ZIP files.

06Which output formats can I save?＋

3D STEP (model for mechanical design collaboration), preview PNG (image for review and sharing) and the Excel placement report (placement list plus no-3D, height-overrun and outside-outline items). The outputs are reference material for design review; compare them with the original design before manufacturing.

07Can I use it without the internet?＋

Board files are processed on your PC. According to the user manual, work that only uses models from an installed KiCad or models already saved in the library does not need the internet. Downloading public KiCad models or using [온라인 검색] (Online search) connects to the internet.

08Is it free for company use?＋

Yes. Under the license agreement it is free of charge for both personal and business (commercial) use. You may not sell it or distribute modified versions, and the original installer may be passed on only unmodified and free of charge. The program is provided “as is”.
