01 / Why it matters

More dimension.
Less manual work.

If you have PCB design data but no 3D model with the parts placed, working with mechanical design may mean ordering separate modeling or doing extra work yourself.

  • No 3D model yet

    Design data alone gives mechanical design no 3D model to work with, so separate modeling or extra work may be needed.

  • Finding and fitting every part

    Finding a 3D model for each part, then placing and orienting them one by one, takes time and effort.

  • From design data to STEP

    PCB 3D Builder uses your design data and parts library to help build the model, check and adjust it, and hand it over as STEP. It helps reduce the time and cost of separate modeling.

The model is reference material for design review; mechanical design and a final review are still needed.

02 / Quick Guide

Drop in, check.
Save as STEP.

Six steps, shown with real screens of the program (4.0). Click a picture to enlarge it. The program interface is in Korean.

What to put in

  • Most accurateASC

    A single PADS ASCII file gives the outline, parts, pads, silkscreen and traces.

  • Without ASCGerber + placement

    Just the Gerbers and placement file everyone gets for PCB orders and SMT. The placement file can be Excel, CSV or TXT.

  • Without GerbersDXF + placement

    A mechanical DXF and a placement file give the board and part placement.

Placement files it reads

SMT placement files (pick & place · centroid · component locations) differ by CAD, but column names and separators are recognized from the content. No need to rename files or convert them to Excel.

FormatExtensionExamples
Excel.xlsx.xlsPlacement data from SMT houses and vendors
CSV.csv.txtAltium Pick and Place · KiCad · OrCAD/Allegro · PADS export
Text report.txtP-CAD Component Locations · Altium Pick and Place (text)
Space-separated text.txt.posKiCad .pos · Allegro place_txt
Eagle mount file.mnt.mnbEagle (no header line)

Only reference, X and Y are required; rotation, side, footprint and value are used when present. Units (mm/mil), origin and Y direction are matched to the Gerbers automatically.

PCB 3D in six steps

  1. 01

    Drop in the files

    Drop the whole delivery folder or ZIP from your vendor. ASC, Gerber, placement and BOM files are told apart by their content.

  2. 02

    Read and matched automatically

    Outline, drills and pads are read, and the origin, Y axis, units and rotation rules are matched automatically, with the current task and time left shown.

  3. 03

    Check the board

    Just confirm the outline reference, board thickness and enclosure height limit. Parts taller than the limit are outlined in red.

  4. 04

    Fill in part 3D automatically

    Real models are found in the KiCad 3D library and fitted to the pads; without a match, the package is built from its dimensions. Parts that are not packages, such as connectors, need a STEP file.

  5. 05

    Check and adjust

    Overlay the 3D model and the pads to confirm pin 1 and polarity, and fix them in a few clicks if needed. On Gerber boards pin 1 is estimated from the silkscreen, so do check it.

  6. 06

    Save as STEP

    Save a STEP ready for your mechanical CAD, with silkscreen, copper and part names. You can keep working while it saves.

Each step is explained in detail in the user guide. Example board on screen: KiCad example ‘RoyalBlue54L Feather’ (CERN-OHL-P v2)

03 / Features

Models filled in.
Less work by hand.

  • Model Matching

    Model matching · auto alignment

    [3D auto-fill] fills parts with real KiCad models, then generated packages, then standard chip shapes, then simple pad-based models. KiCad models are found by footprint, part number, package code, online part data and pad layout, even when the name has no package in it. With ASC input, IC pin 1 and diode polarity are aligned too.

  • Package Generation

    Automatic package generation

    Parts with no KiCad model are built from a package dictionary (SOIC · SSOP · TSSOP · SOT · QFN · DFN · LQFP · SOD · SMA/SMB/SMC · PowerPAK · electrolytic capacitors · power inductors · crystals/oscillators and more): the package that fits the pads is chosen and leads are placed on the pads.

  • Check & Adjust

    Part check & adjust

    Double-click a part to compare its 3D model with the pads, pin 1 pad and board top surface, then rotate it by 90° or 180°, enter coordinates, drag it, or drop it onto the board surface. Adjustments are saved per footprint in the library and reused on the next board.

  • Per Reference

    Per-reference settings

    Give one part its own 3D or adjustment, or remove it from the 3D, while other parts with the same footprint stay as they are. Useful when the same footprint sits on the top and bottom at the same spot and the 3D appears twice. The ASC file is not changed.

  • Free Placement

    3D free placement

    Place parts that are not in the ASC (STEP · ZIP) anywhere on the board to check a concept or clearance, for example a sub board mounted on the main board. They are included in the height and outside-board checks and in the STEP.

  • Board Preview

    Board preview

    Top, front and side views are shown together; switch between top and bottom (mirrored). Release an area zoom with ESC or a single click, and click a part to see its reference, height and side.

  • Gerber Input

    Boards from Gerber + placement files

    No ASC needed: the Gerbers you send for PCB orders and the SMT placement file are enough, from any CAD. Copper, exposed pads and silkscreen go in exactly as in the Gerbers. Drop a whole folder or ZIP and the Gerbers, placement file and BOM are found.

  • STEP Options

    Board surface · color · labels

    With ASC input, the STEP can include silkscreen, copper and traces. Choose the PCB color and whether to engrave part names (none · selected · all).

  • Height & Report

    Height check · review report

    Enter the enclosure’s inner height to outline taller parts in red; parts outside the board are outlined in yellow. The Excel report lists placement, parts without 3D, height overruns and parts outside the board, gathering what to check after the model is built.

06 / FAQ

Questions before you start.
Answered one by one.

01Which design files do I need?＋

A single PADS ASCII (.asc) file is recommended. Without ASC, use the Gerbers from your PCB order with the SMT placement file, or DXF (top · bottom) with a placement file (Excel · CSV) if needed. A BOM and part STEP · ZIP files are optional extras that help.

02I design in a CAD other than PADS. Can I use it?＋

Yes. From 4.0, any CAD works with the Gerbers from your PCB order and the SMT placement file, and copper and silkscreen go into the STEP exactly as in the Gerbers. Gerbers have no pin numbers, so IC pin 1 and diode polarity are estimated from the silkscreen; check parts marked “pin 1 check”. DXF with a placement file also works, but then the STEP has the board and parts without silkscreen or copper.

03Is a BOM required?＋

No, it is optional. It improves finding part model candidates by part number.

04Are all parts built accurately and automatically?＋

No. 3D auto-fill uses real KiCad models, then generated packages, then standard chip shapes, then simple pad-based models and aligns them to the pads, but some parts may remain without 3D or need a check. These are mostly parts such as connectors and switches that need a STEP file. Drag a STEP from [온라인 검색] (Online search) onto the part’s row, or adjust it in the check & adjust view. Parts you do not need can also be removed (saved per board, restorable any time).

05I was using an earlier version. How do I update?＋

Run the new installer without removing the old version; it installs over it. The parts library (Documents\PCB3D_Library), settings and project files are kept. Coming from 2.x, open an ASC and run [3D auto-fill] once: parts created automatically before are searched again with the new rules, while parts you assigned or adjusted stay as they are. “3D from PDF” was removed in 3.0.

06Which output formats can I save?＋

3D STEP (model for mechanical design collaboration), preview PNG (image for review and sharing) and the Excel placement report (placement list plus no-3D, height-overrun and outside-board items). Work in progress is saved as a project file (.p3d). The outputs are reference material for design review; compare them with the original design before manufacturing.

07Can I use it without the internet?＋

Board files are processed on your PC, and board files and design data are never sent out. The internet is needed to download new KiCad models, for [온라인 검색] (Online search), and for the online part check (only part numbers are sent; it can be turned off in Settings). Behind a company firewall, allow gitlab.com · raw.githubusercontent.com · jlcpcb.com or install KiCad on the PC.

08Is it free for company use?＋

Yes. Under the license agreement it is free of charge for both personal and business (commercial) use. You may not sell it or distribute modified versions, and the original installer may be passed on only unmodified and free of charge. The program is provided “as is”.

If installation is blocked When “Windows protected your PC” appears or setup does not start＋

PCB 3D Builder has no code signature, so Windows asks for confirmation with a blue window the first time it runs. This is a normal warning, not a problem with the program. Only use an installer downloaded from this page.

When the blue “Windows protected your PC” window appears

  1. Click the [More info] link in the blue window.
  2. The window changes to show “App: PCB3D_Builder_Setup_….exe” and “Publisher: Unknown publisher”, and a [Run anyway] button appears below.
  3. Click [Run anyway] to start the installation.

[Don’t run] simply closes the window. Always click [More info] first.

When there is no [Run anyway] button after [More info]

The company PC blocks it with a security policy.

  • Option 1: right-click the downloaded exe → [Properties] → check “Security: Unblock” at the bottom → [OK], then run it again.
  • Option 2: if it still does not run, ask that PC’s IT administrator to allow it. Users cannot change this setting themselves.

Other blocks

  • The browser blocks the download: if Edge or Chrome says the file “isn’t commonly downloaded” and does not keep it, open the file’s menu (…) in the download list and choose [Keep].
  • Antivirus removes the file: restore it from quarantine or add an exception. If the antivirus is managed by your company, ask your IT administrator.
  • No administrator rights: choose “Install for current user only” as the installation scope.
